# The Process of Getting to My Final Story

## From Part I to Part II

In Part I of this project, I decided to build my story around the idea that homelessness is more than just a number, and I kept this central idea throughout each part of the project. I followed my initial outline closely throughout all stages, developing many of the visualizations I planned, and focusing heavily on the human stories. 

I did make some changes between Part I and Part II. Specifically, I decided to move away from questions of shelter capacity, and from making all of my visualizations/thematic points about comparisons over time. While I did keep some time series comparisons to establish the scale of the issue, I used these to lead into a focus of recent homelessness (2024 specifically). I made this decision because 2024 had such high levels of all types of homelessness compared to past years. Per TA feedback on Part I, I also decided to add analysis on homelessness by geographical area, which strengthened my point that this issue is widespread everywhere in this country and requires intervention.

My original intent had been to present an informative rather than persuasive story, but these changes naturally led to me developing a call-to-action. This shift inspired me to include more structural analysis in the story, pointing out contributing factors of homelessness and highlighting the insufficient and counterproductive current policy responses.

## From Part II to Part III

### Changes to My Target Audience

In the first parts of the project, I imagined the audience to be both community advocates and policymakers who want to better understand homelessness and how to address it. However, not having timely access to policymakers led me to interview a group of policy and public affairs students. While these students will become the policymakers of the future, their current role is more similar to potential advocates/everyday community members. The reactions of these respondents also prompted me to consider who I wanted my audience to be. Many respondents were shocked and emotionally moved by my story, and left it with new understandings and new commitments to supporting better policy responses to homelessness. Therefore, I think my story is best suited to these types of audiences, ones who may not have the prior knowledge and power that a policymaker does, but whose position as community members gives them a deeper connection to the issue, and can inspire a deeper commitment to advocacy. In short, my audience are the people I will actually be presenting to in class. 

### Changes to My Call-to-Action

The professor suggested I tighten my call to action and make it more actionable. I also wanted to adjust it to more accurately reflect my new target audience. So I was specific in the policies my audience needs to support once they become decisionmakers. Combining the professor’s feedback and feedback from one of my interviews, I also added some resource links at the end of the story for my audience to begin making changes now. 

### Final Design Changes and Decisions 

Throughout this class, I have developed a very minimalistic and simplistic design language. Personally, I understand straightforward data visualizations much easier than complex ones. As my intended audience shaped up to be much more general, it was important to me to maintain this thread of simplicity throughout the final project. In both the data visualizations and other elements, I wanted my story to be widely accessible because I wanted every community member to have the opportunity to understand and become deeply invested in these issues. This commitment was further motivated by my interviews, where the majority of respondents responded positively to the simplicity of the visualizations and overall Shorthand story. 

I did make a few design changes based on my interview feedback. The grey theme of the story was successful amongst my respondents for the somber mood it created. Some also suggested I fully lean into that theme by removing the blue backgrounds behind my data visualizations, and by saving color to make an impact. I decided to use blue text to emphasize important pieces of information, such as the large number of people experiencing homelessness in 2024.

Another change I implemented, inspired by respondent feedback, was redesigning my map of homelessness distribution across the country in 2024. The original map compared overall homelessness, but this created much less visual contrast between the states because California and New York have vastly greater populations than the other states. Per respondent feedback, I changed the map to represent homelessness per 1,000 residents, which actually revealed that some states had greater prevalence of homelessness than the states that had previously appeared as outliers. 

The TA and some respondents had difficulty reading some of the text against the background images, so I ensured all text had a solid background separating it from the photographs to improve readability. Another TA design suggestion I implemented was turning some of my textual statistics into simple data visualizations. For this, I mainly used Infogram to create some progress donuts (with small animations to improve the engagement of my story, which is something a respondent had recommended as well) and I created some infographics to condense the large blocks of textual information (this was to make it easier for me to present to my classmates, too). For the 2024 PIT count number, I used Canva to create a rapid counter animation. 

# Final Thoughts: Reflections and What I Would Do in the Future

Overall, this was a huge learning experience. Prior to this class, I had never used Tableau or made my own data visualization. I had never gone into a large dataset and cleaned it up for my own use. Prior to this project, I had never used Shorthand. Another notable and surprising part of my experience was seeing how quickly my story’s narrative revealed itself once I actually began to explore the data, and solicit user feedback. One component of my project that I would like to revisit and build upon is my initial idea to create a visualization depicting the differences between racial and ethnic groups in homelessness experiences. I initially tried to create a simple visualization comparing raw counts, but similar to my map, this did not depict the disparities in the way I hoped. I ultimately did not have the time to find more data to depict proportional differences, so I cut that from my story.
